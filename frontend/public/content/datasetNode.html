1. What is a dataset?

A dataset is a structured collection of examples. In supervised learning, each row is an observation; columns describe it or record a known outcome. A model learns associations from labeled examples to predict an outcome for a new example.

2. Observations, features, and targets

Each student is an observation. Features are inputs such as study hours. The target variable is the outcome to predict; its known values are labels, available during supervised training.

Think: Why should Result not also be a predictor when the goal is to predict Result?

3. Classification versus regression

Classification predicts categories: Pass/Fail or flower species. Regression predicts a quantity: examination score or temperature. A numeric 0/1 label can still denote classification. ML-Tutor’s current workflows are classification-oriented.

4. Dataset suitability and quality

Check relevance, data types, missing or inconsistent values, sample size, representativeness, and target-class distribution. A dataset from a limited population may not generalize to a broader population.

  • Features should be available when a prediction is needed.
  • Investigate blanks, duplicates, impossible values, and inconsistent units.
  • Ask who and what situations the rows include or exclude.

5. Understanding class distribution

If 85 observations are Pass and 15 are Fail, an always-Pass classifier gets 85% accuracy while finding no failing students. That is a baseline, not proof that a distribution is good or bad.

6. Training and testing data

Reserve observations as test data to evaluate generalization to examples the model did not train on. The split trades training examples against an informative evaluation sample.

7. Understanding data leakage

Target leakage: predicting examination results before an exam should not use a final grade calculated afterward. Preprocessing leakage: learn transformation statistics from training data, then apply them to held-out test data.

Question: Which may be inappropriate before an exam: attendance, a final grade, or study hours? Explain.
